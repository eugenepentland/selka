      "U1"
      "TPS23757 — WHY THIS PART, WHAT IT REPLACES, AND WHAT THE BOARD STILL OWES.

WHAT IT REPLACES. The non-isolated predecessor, lib/modules/poe-pd-tps2378.sexp, needed an EXTERNAL converter and an explicit handshake between them: the TPS2378's open-drain CDB pin had to hold the downstream buck off through the 140 mA inrush, with a pull-up the buck module had to supply and a polarity that one sentence of the TPS2378 datasheet actually states backwards. That interface is gone. The TPS23757 contains both halves, and the interlock is internal: 'Converter switching is disabled while C charges and V falls from V to nearly V' (TPS23757.pdf p.18). There is no PG port on this module and no enable glue for the board to get wrong.

WHAT IS ON WHICH SIDE OF THE BARRIER. Everything in this module except D_SEC, C_OUT1/2/3, U3, R_LED, R_TLBIAS, R_FBU, R_FBL, R_IZ, C_IZ and the LED half of U2 is PRIMARY, floating with the PoE return. That includes the controller, its V_C and V_B rails and the bias winding — the bias winding is on the primary side of the transformer's insulation system, which is exactly what the datasheet's isolation spec says: '1500 Vrms one minute isolation primary AND BIAS to secondary' (POE13F-12L.pdf p.1).

STARTUP SEQUENCE, all internal (TPS23757.pdf p.18-19):
  1. PSE detects the 24.9 k signature, then reads the 90.9 Ohm Class 3 signature.
  2. V_DD - V_SS rises through the 33.9/35/36.1 V PoE UVLO; the hotswap MOSFET turns on with a 140 mA inrush limit; converter switching is INHIBITED.
  3. Meanwhile the internal bootstrap source charges C_VC from VDD1 at 2.7/4.8/6.8 mA. 15.8 uF to 9 V takes about 36 ms.
  4. Inrush completes (20 uF x 48 V / 140 mA = 6.9 ms), the PD switches to its ~465 mA operational limit.
  5. V_C passes 9 V, the bootstrap source turns off, the internal ~4 ms softstart ramps the peak-current demand, and the bias winding takes V_C over before C_VC falls 3.5 V.
  6. The secondary rises, U3 and U2 take the loop, CTL leaves its pull-up.
  On a shorted output, step 5 fails and the part hiccups at about 48 Hz with a 33 % duty — the intended behaviour, not a fault.

WHAT THE BOARD STILL OWES.
  * The four CT_PAIR* taps in the board's own high-voltage net class (this module cannot class a port's post-bridging net).
  * The two-plane split: a LOCAL VSS plane carrying C_VDD, D3 and R_CLS, and a SEPARATE local RTN/ARTN/COM plane carrying the converter primary. They differ by only the hotswap MOSFET's 0.43 Ohm, so a careless merged pour silently defeats the hotswap, and [ARTN, COM] to RTN has a +/-2 V ABSOLUTE MAXIMUM.
  * The isolation gap itself. The poe-primary net class asks for 2 mm; the board must also keep the transformer's own primary and secondary pin rows apart (they are on opposite 21.97 mm sides of the package, which is the reason for choosing a part with that pinout), route no secondary copper under the primary, and treat C_Y's two pads as the single controlled crossing.
  * Placement per TI's stated pin rules: R_BLNK at pin 18, R_FRS at pin 19, C_VC3 at pin 6, R_CS-to-CS short and away from the gate drive.

BRING-UP, and the loop measurement is not optional. Measure: the PoE start-up sequence at V_C and VOUT from a real PSE and from a 37 V and a 57 V bench supply; the drain waveform at both input corners and at full load, confirming the clamp holds V_DS under 130 V and that the ring is damped; the output ripple at 0.6 A; the LOOP GAIN by injection at the output, at 0.15 A and 0.6 A, confirming the crossover and phase margin the header predicts from a model that has never been validated on hardware; the efficiency at both corners, which replaces the deliberately pessimistic 0.78 declared on the VOUT port; and the isolation, by a 1500 Vrms hipot from the RJ-45 shell to board ground with C_Y temporarily lifted.")

      "C_Y"
      "ISOLATION BARRIER — READ BEFORE MOVING ANYTHING.

This board has exactly THREE crossings of the primary/secondary barrier and they are all in this module:
  1. T1, the transformer, rated 1500 Vrms for one minute, primary AND bias winding to secondary (POE13F-12L.pdf p.1). Its primary and bias leads are pins 1, 2, 3, 11, 12 on one side of the package and its secondary leads are pins 5, 6, 7, 8 on the other, 16.88 mm of land-pattern gap apart — that separation is a reason the part was chosen.
  2. U2, the optocoupler: LED on the secondary, phototransistor on the primary.
  3. C_Y, this part: 2.2 nF / 2 kV from primary RTN to secondary GND.
NOTHING ELSE MAY CROSS. The (net-class \"poe-primary\" … (keepout 2.0)) form gives every primary net a 2 mm halo that secondary copper may not enter on the same layer and that no through-via may pass through, and drc-net-class-keepout reports any violation. C_Y's two pads are the deliberate exception: they sit in that halo by construction, and a reviewer looking at a keepout finding on C_Y should confirm it is C_Y and then look no further.

WHY A Y-CAPACITOR AT ALL. Without it the transformer's interwinding capacitance still couples the switching edge across the barrier — it just has to return through whatever path exists, which for a PoE PD is the Ethernet cable. That is a conducted-emissions failure (CISPR 22 treats the cable as a telecommunication port; IEEE 802.3at 33.3/33.4 additionally limit injected noise) and it is the failure mode TI's SLUA469 is written about. 2.2 nF at 253 kHz is 286 Ohm, low enough to be the preferred return; at 60 Hz it is 1.2 MOhm, so no meaningful touch current.

WHAT THIS BARRIER IS AND IS NOT. It is FUNCTIONAL isolation to the 1500 Vrms type test IEEE 802.3 specifies for a PD, which is what lets the board's SMA outer conductors, USB-C shell and enclosure be at a potential unrelated to the PoE return. It is NOT reinforced mains insulation: C_Y is a 2 kV DC-rated MLCC, not an agency-certified Y1/Y2 part, and the transformer's rating is a one-minute type test rather than a continuous working-voltage classification. A product whose safety file needs reinforced insulation across this barrier must substitute a certified Y2 capacitor and re-qualify the transformer; that is a product decision, recorded here rather than assumed away.")
