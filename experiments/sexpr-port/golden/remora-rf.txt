      "Single-input power tree. V_12V_ISO arrives already regulated on J1 pins 1-4 from the base board's isolated PoE flyback (11.80-12.30 V measured at the base's secondary; the buck's (vin 12) case is sized for a wider 11.4-12.6 V and bounds it) → LM5164 synchronous buck → V_7V5 → two LT3045 LDOs → the negative charge pump. The lever, if it is ever wanted, is the intermediate rail: dropping the buck from 7.4766 V to about 7.0 V would still leave 0.3 V of LT3045 dropout margin at the 6.77 V low corner and would return about 0.19 W.       "Single calibration input fanned to all four channels by a BINARY TREE of three ADP-2-20+ 2-way 0-degree splitters (20-2000 MHz, case CD542): cal_root's SUM takes CAL_IN, its two outputs feed the SUM ports of cal_a and cal_b, and those four outputs feed the four switches. Every leg is the same two hops, which is the whole point: leg matching becomes a placement property instead of four meanders. LEVEL PLAN: 3.7 dB typical of total loss per stage (3.5 typ / 3.8 max below 200 MHz, 3.7 typ / 4.0 max from 200-1000 MHz) gives 7.4 dB typical and 7.6-8.0 dB worst case for the tree, plus 0.1 dB in each of the two DC blocks a cal path crosses. Antenna path gain is about +21.7 dB with a 1.7 dB noise figure; the cal path is about +14.2 dB, i.e. 7.5 dB lower, so a cal tone must be driven about 7.5 dB harder than an equivalent antenna signal. LIMITS at CAL_IN: keep the tone at or below +7 dBm for specified linearity (the PMA3-14LN+ reaches its +22 dBm output P1dB at about +7.7 dBm here) and never above +20 dBm, which is the amplifier's +12 dBm continuous absolute-maximum input referred back through the tree, the switch and the blocks. The M3SWA2's 24 dBm per-output-port ceiling (+31.6 dBm at CAL_IN) and the root splitter's own 1 W rating (+30.1 dBm at CAL_IN) are both above that, so the amplifier is the binding limit on every port. ISOLATION: 18 dB typ / 15 dB MIN between the two legs that share a second-stage splitter (1-2 and 3-4) and about 25/22 dB across the branches; the 15 dB minimum is flat across the whole band and is 3 dB worse than the four-way part it replaces below 400 MHz. UNBALANCE: <= 0.3 dB / 3.0 deg between legs sharing a second-stage part and <= 0.6 dB / 6.0 deg across the branches over 200-1000 MHz — roughly half the four-way's 0.9 dB / 11 deg at the top of the band. PORT MATCH is the budget that got worse: the ADP-2-20+ publishes no VSWR specification at all and its typical data shows the SUM port at a flat 1.97-2.01:1 (9.5 dB return loss) across the band, against the four-way's 1.04-1.13:1 below 300 MHz, so a calibration source without its own output pad or isolator sees a 2:1 load everywhere rather than only at the top of the band. The calibration measures the resulting ripple out because every channel shares the same sum path. AMBIENT: the ADP-2-20+'s -40 to +85 degC operating case range is still the tightest temperature limit on the board and bounds the product's ambient envelope. LAYOUT: match the two first-stage links (root PORT2 to cal_a SUM, root PORT1 to cal_b SUM - the outputs are swapped relative to the obvious order because the packages are on the BOTTOM face, where the mirrored land makes PORT2 the northern output) to each other and the four second-stage legs to each other — the two `(match-group …)` declarations below do exactly that — because any length difference is a fixed phase error the array calibration will absorb as a real antenna phase and then apply to live signals. The whole tree - the three packages, the five DC blocks and every leg - is on B.Cu, so a first-stage link carries NO via and a second-stage leg carries exactly ONE, the barrel up into the switch's RF_OUT2 pad (see the rf-cal route wave); keep the via count and the bend count identical on all four, not just the length."))
