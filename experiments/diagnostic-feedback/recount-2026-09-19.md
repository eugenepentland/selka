# Historical recount of the pre-M0 study

The first study measured repair rounds by regex over whole transcripts, which
counted assistant prose and repeated deliveries of the same result. This recount
uses the transcripts' actual structure — matched `tool/call` → `tool/result`
pairs whose command invoked the compiler, deduplicated by call id — and it states
what the old evidence cannot support. Raw data: `recount.json`.

| condition | sessions | transcripts | matched compiler results | of those, with diagnostics | solved |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| text | 24 | 24 | 186 | 52 | 24 |
| structured | 24 | 24 | 194 | 52 | 24 |
| fix | 24 | 24 | 185 | 45 | 24 |
| oneshot | 24 | 24 | 112 | 7 | 19 |

All 96 transcripts survived, so nothing had to be estimated from a missing log.
The counts are lower than the published repair-round medians (2.5 / 3.0 / 1.5)
because a transcript-wide regex counted prose and repeated reads; the recount is
the defensible number.

**Limits, stated rather than papered over.** Per-process invocation boundaries are
not reconstructable where several compiler calls shared one shell command, and a
result body containing diagnostics cannot be split into one event per diagnostic.
Those quantities are therefore reported as *tool results containing diagnostics*,
never as exact compiler invocations. The twelve re-runs of the corrected
`unknown-name` fault sit beside their predecessors in `runs/`; the first-pass
attempts remain as the record of the defective task.

The M0 instrument removes all three limitations going forward: events are written
by the wrapper per compiler process, so boundaries, outcomes, source hashes and
diagnostic identities are recorded rather than reconstructed.
