# M2 live-envelope pilot — 8 participant sessions on the development cases (2026-09-19)

This is the mechanism pilot for the M2 diagnostic-feedback token envelope. It asks
one question: **can a task-level generated-token budget be enforced on a real
headless participant while it works, without losing or inventing a single token?**
It is not the M2 treatment comparison; the treatments have not been run yet.

The runner is `experiments/diagnostic-feedback-m2/enforcing_runner.py`
(sha256 `52018fe9c5197cecf67332bdf159bc560d55a15af4659236772886665aa4c955`).
`budget.py` is the frozen contract and was not modified; `m2_usage.py`'s semantics
were not modified. Nothing was written under `experiments/diagnostic-feedback/` or
`experiments/cascade/` (verified below).

## 1. What was built and how it enforces

`enforcing_runner.py` launches one participant at a time with the M1 working-copy
layout (`run.py`'s build, wrapper, event log, per-run Zig caches inside the working
copy) and then watches that session's harness log **while it runs**:

* every ~2 s the whole growing `session.v3.jsonl.zstd` is re-decompressed through
  the verified reader `m2_usage.read_calls` (same `cwd` match, same 5 s start-time
  slack, torn final frames skipped and counted);
* each model call seen for the first time is charged once, deduplicated by
  `(session path, call index)`, with `allowance = envelope.remaining()` at the
  moment of the charge;
* on `refused` (an overshoot) the participant's **process group** is terminated —
  SIGTERM, then SIGKILL after a 5 s grace — and the candidate is recorded as
  ended-by-overshoot. A call written after the overshoot is still real spend, so it
  is charged too and flagged `after_overshoot`; `spent` always equals the sum of
  what was generated. Nothing is retried or truncated;
* after the process stops the watcher keeps reading for up to 3 s until the log
  stops growing, so calls flushed during shutdown are charged as well;
* one envelope per task in both policies; `repair` runs one session, `independent`
  starts another session only while `remaining() > 0` and the pool bound allows;
* selection is by the **public** check only. The hidden manifest is run once, on the
  selected candidate, through `run.py`'s own grading path, and never informs
  selection;
* the generated-token budget is stated in the prompt, and the prompt text plus its
  sha256 are frozen in `cohorts/pilot-5000/<case>/<policy>/identity.json`.

The prompt is the M1 treatment prompt with one added constraint bullet:

> - You have {cap} generated (output) tokens for this task, reasoning included; the
>   harness reads this session's log while it runs and terminates the attempt as
>   soon as that budget is exceeded.

The policy is *not* mentioned in the prompt, so repair and independent participants
saw identical text for a case (the two policies share a prompt sha256, see §5).

Evidence per cell is `cohorts/pilot-5000/<case>/<policy>/`: `record.json` (charges,
totals, candidates, termination, selection, acceptance, missing observations),
`identity.json` (frozen prompt + sha), `prompt.md`, `sealed/<label>/` (the graded
snapshot), `logs/<label>-<session>.zstd` (raw session log copy), `<label>.events.jsonl`
(compiler-invocation events), `<label>.stdout.txt` / `.stderr.txt`, and the per-call
usage evidence inside `record.json`.

## 4. Did the stated budget change pacing?

**This pilot cannot answer that, and it does not claim to.** The four development
cases have **no prior trajectories** — no session had ever run on them before this
pilot — so there is no unbudgeted measurement of the same cases to compare against,
and the pilot has no no-budget control arm either. Both facts mean the comparison
does not exist by construction.

**Correction 2026-09-19 (post-freeze; no number above changes).** The "no prior
trajectories" claim holds only for `dev-generic`. Three of these four dev cases are
byte-identical to cases in the M1b test split — `dev-bool` ≡ `bool-a`, `dev-checked` ≡
`checked-a`, `dev-variant` ≡ `variant-a`/`variant-b` (identical `project/`, `public/`,
`hidden/` and `request.md`; their prompt shas in §5 equal the `*-a` cases') — so prior
M1b trajectories of those exact tasks do exist, and the figures below that match dev
cases to M1b families are describing the same instances, not merely the same family.
The pilot's spend numbers therefore describe some of the tasks the frozen M2 design
will run (13 distinct cases after the duplicate `variant-b` was dropped); they are not
an independent sample. Nothing in the pilot's mechanism conclusions depends on this,
and no session needs re-running.

For context only, and *not* as a matched comparison, the M2 calibration pool (the 112
finished M1b sessions, measured under **no** token budget) has a median of 3,249
generated tokens per session (p75 5,207; 82/112 sessions fit whole inside 5,000), and
its `text` arm — M2's treatment match — a median of 3,327 (21/28 fit in 5,000). The
M1b cases closest in family to these dev cases spent, per session: `bool-int` 2,096
median (text arm 1,810), `checked-result` 5,061 (5,292), `generic-requirement` 3,187
(3,343), `variant-coverage` 1,843 (1,986). The pilot's cells spent 750–2,592. Dev
cases are different, newly authored instances of those families with the same shape
(2 public inputs, 4 hidden inputs, the same diagnostic code as their family), so any
difference here mixes case difficulty with the budget statement. The cap never bound
(largest spend 2,592 = 51.8 % of the cap; the cohort used 33.6 % of its 8 × 5,000
allowance), so even the *mechanical* pressure of the budget was near-absent.

## 6. Live enforcement versus the replay's policy

`envelope_replay.py` models enforcement over trajectories that were measured without
a budget; this runner enforces it. Two differences matter:

1. **Look-ahead.** The replay can inspect a candidate trajectory's first call and
   refuse to start a candidate the envelope cannot afford ("first call … exceeds
   remaining"). Live, a call's size is unknown until it has been generated, so a
   candidate is started whenever `remaining() > 0` and its *first* call may itself
   overshoot, ending the candidate immediately. The live policy is therefore weakly
   more expensive per started candidate than the replayed one, and it can end a
   candidate on the very first call.
2. **Disclosure and timing.** The replay's caps were never shown to the agents it
   replays; here the cap is in the prompt and the kill is real, mid-session. Neither
   the pacing effect of disclosure nor the behavioural effect of a real termination
   exists in the replay.

One further pilot-specific difference: `independent` ran **one candidate per cell**,
because the approved budget of 8 participant sessions buys one session per cell
(4 cases × 2 policies). The multi-candidate independent loop — starting a second
candidate while the envelope still has budget, sharing one envelope across
candidates, and stopping when it is exhausted — is therefore demonstrated only in
the dry run (§2), not on real participants. Consequently each independent cell's
`stop_reason` is `session-budget` (the pilot's one-session-per-cell allowance), not
`envelope-exhausted`: the envelopes had 2,408–4,250 generated tokens left when the
cell stopped.

## 7. Missing or failed observations

* **Missing session logs: none.** 8/8 matched, 0 malformed lines, 0 missing-spend
  records; every record has an empty `missing` list and an empty
  `mechanism_failures` list.
* **The refusal→kill path was never exercised on a real participant.** No session
  overshot the cap, so no live participant was terminated by the harness. The kill
  branch is verified against stand-in process groups (dry run) and a SIGTERM-ignoring
  child (selftest), and the live *observation/charging* branch is verified on all 64
  real calls — but their composition on a real dsh session is unobserved. This is the
  single most important gap.
* **Multi-candidate `independent`: not observed live** (see §6).
* **No retries, no infrastructure failures, no host failures**; no session hit the
  invocation limit; the wrapper logged 0 infrastructure failures.
* **One bash-tool audit invocation was cut off by a 60 s tool timeout** while
  auditing `dev-variant/repair`; it was re-run alone and passed 13/13. No evidence
  was affected.

## 8. What this does not establish

* **Not a policy comparison.** With one candidate per cell, `repair` and
  `independent` are not distinguished here; no claim about which policy spends or
  selects better is supported.
* **Not a treatment result.** All eight cells ran the `text` arm only; the M2 arms
  have not been compared.
* **Not a pacing or budget-effect result.** No unbudgeted trajectories exist for
  these cases and no no-budget control was run, so the statement in the prompt
  cannot be credited or blamed for any difference (§4).
* **Not evidence that spend is bounded by the cap.** An overshoot is real spend by
  construction: the harness can only kill *after* the overshooting call's usage line
  is written, so the observed bound is `cap + one call`. Observed lag was 0.2–2.0 s
  here; on a slow or large call the overshoot would be correspondingly larger.
* **Not a variance estimate.** One session per cell, four small single-file dev
  cases, one repetition — no variance, no case generalisation, no hard-case
  behaviour.
* **Not a quality statement about the fixes.** Acceptance is the module's public and
  hidden grading of the sealed snapshot (4 + 4 checks); passing it means the fix
  satisfies those checks, nothing more.
* **Not a statement about uninterrupted long sessions.** Sessions here were 14–28 s
  and 5–12 calls; enforcement was never tested across a long, many-call session,
  where polling cost, partial-frame handling and the settle window would matter more.

## 9. Verdict: trustworthy enough to scale?

**For what was measured, yes; for the full M2 comparison, not yet — one deliberate
low-cap cell should exercise the live kill first.** The evidence supports:

* live per-call charging that is exact, idempotent and auditable end to end
  (8/8 records, 104/104 audit checks, per-call sums recomputed from raw logs);
* correct handling of a growing, frame-appended zstd log during the session
  (64/64 calls charged live, 0 malformed, 0 lost or invented calls);
* sealing, pruning, one-at-a-time execution, the 8-session budget and the
  mechanism-failure stop rule working as specified;
* the budget genuinely reaching the participant (in every session log).

The gap is that the enforcement has never had to fire on a real participant. Before
scaling to the full M2 comparison, run at least one cell at a cap low enough to force
an overshoot (the observed per-call sizes here reach 951 generated tokens, so a cap
of ~1,000 on a task that needs several calls would fire early), confirm
`killed_by_harness=true`, `late_calls_after_stop=0`, the recalled exit signal, and
that the overshooting call is charged at its actual spend — then scale. A scaling run
should also decide the `independent` candidate budget per cell (and therefore the
session budget), since one candidate per cell cannot distinguish the policies.
